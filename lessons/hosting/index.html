Hosting: from your kitchen to the shop window

By the end, Lizzie can say what hosting is, and she will have put a tiny website of her own on the internet with Vercel, with a link anyone can open on their phone.

Before class

about 10 min

The idea

10 min

A website on your laptop is like a cake in your kitchen. Only people in your house can see it.

Hosting is putting it in a shop window, so anyone in the world can come and see it, any time.

Vercel is the shop. It keeps your website on its computers, which are always on, and gives it an address.

Say it like this: “Your laptop goes to sleep. Vercel's computers never do. That's why your site needs a host.”

Hosting wordIn the bakery
HostingPutting the cake in a shop window for everyone
Host (Vercel)The shop that's always open
Address (URL)The shop's address, like lilelus.vercel.app
DeployCarrying the cake to the shop and putting it out
LiveIt's in the window now. People can see it.

She already knows a hosted site: mum's. When she merged her pull requests this week, Vercel put her changes in the window for her.

The golden rule: a hosted site is public. Anyone can see it. No full name, school, home address, phone number or photos of faces.

The build

40 min

Before every click, Lizzie guesses what will happen. She does the clicking. You ask the questions.

  1. step 1 · 5 min

    Kitchen or shop?

    She does
    1. Open lilelus.vercel.app on the laptop, then on the phone.
    2. Look at the address bar on both.
    She checks
    • It works on both, because it's hosted.

    Guess“If a website is only saved on your laptop, can mum open it on her phone?” (No. It's still in the kitchen. That's why we need hosting.)

  2. step 2 · 10 min

    Make a tiny site

    She does
    1. Make a folder on the desktop called my-first-site.
    2. Inside it, make a file called index.html and paste the starter below. (Or ask Claude: “Make me a one-page website about my favourite cakes, in one file called index.html.”)
    3. Change the words to her own.
    4. Double-click index.html to open it.
    She checks
    • The page opens.
    • The address bar starts with file://. That means it's still in the kitchen.
    <!doctype html>
<html>
<head>
  <meta charset="utf-8">
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1">
  <title>My favourite cakes</title>
  <style>
    body { font-family: sans-serif; background: #fff4f6; text-align: center; padding: 40px; }
    h1 { color: #b5375b; }
  </style>
</head>
<body>
  <h1>My favourite cakes</h1>
  <p>Hi! This is my first website on the internet.</p>
  <p>My top three: chocolate, red velvet and lemon.</p>
</body>
</html>

    Guess“Why must the file be called index.html?” (It's the front door. When someone visits the address, the host opens index.html first.)

  3. step 3 · 10 min

    Put it in the shop window

    She does
    1. Go to vercel.com/drop.
    2. Drag the my-first-site folder onto the page.
    3. Give the project a name, like lizzies-cakes. Then click Deploy.
    4. Open the link it gives her, on the laptop and on the phone.
    She checks
    • The address now ends in .vercel.app, not file://.
    • It works on the phone. Send it to mum!

    Guess“If you close your laptop now, will the site still work on the phone?” (Yes! It lives on Vercel's computers now, not on the laptop. Try it.)

  4. step 4 · 10 min

    Change the window display

    She does
    1. Change something in index.html, like a new colour or a new favourite cake. Save it.
    2. Refresh the .vercel.app link.
    3. In the Vercel dashboard, open her project and drag the folder onto it again.
    4. Refresh the link again.
    She checks
    • The first refresh shows the old page. The second shows the new one.
    • Opens Deployments: each drop is saved there as its own version.

    Guess“You saved the file. Why didn't the live site change straight away?” (You changed the cake in the kitchen. You have to carry it to the shop again: that's deploying.)

  5. step 5 · 5 min

    Compare with mum's site

    You show
    1. On your Vercel dashboard, open the lilelus project → Deployments.
    2. Find the deployments from her merged pull requests this week.
    She spots
    • Nobody dragged a folder for mum's site. Vercel is connected to GitHub, so it deploys by itself every time main changes.

    Guess“Which is easier when you change things often: dragging a folder, or GitHub delivering it for you?” (GitHub. It's like a delivery van that takes every new recipe to the shop by itself.)

If something goes wrong

The link shows “404: Not Found”

Vercel can't find the front door. Check the file is called exactly index.html (not Index.html or index.html.txt), and that it's inside the folder she dropped.

The page shows the code instead of a web page

The file was saved as text, often index.html.txt. In TextEdit on a Mac, use Format → Make Plain Text first, then save as index.html.

Vercel asks her to sign up

The account isn't signed in on this browser. Sign in, then drop the folder again.

The name she wanted is taken

Every .vercel.app address is unique, like a shop address. Add something, like lizzies-cakes-2026.

Hosting words

hosting
Putting your website in a shop window, so anyone can see it.

The company whose computers keep your site open, like Vercel.

Where people find your site, like lilelus.vercel.app.

Sending your site to the host.

Questions

  3. Why does a website need a host, instead of staying on your laptop?
  4. What's one thing you should never put on a public website?
