Assignment · for Lizzie

Build your second website, with Claude

Make a new website about something you love, by telling Claude what to build, one step at a time. Claude is your helper. You decide what goes on your site.

Hand in: your website link

How to work with Claude

  1. Ask for one thing at a time. Small cakes are easier to check than giant ones.
  2. Look at what changed. Open the site and check it does what you asked.
  3. Say yes, or ask again. Not right? Tell Claude what's wrong: “Make the heading bigger and pink.”
  4. Write it down. Copy each prompt into your journal.

1. Pick your topic

Choose anything you like, except cakes (you've done those!). Some ideas:

  • My favourite book or film
  • A hobby I love
  • My dream holiday
  • An animal I find amazing
  • A recipe I can make
  • A game and how to play it

2. Build it, step by step

Each step has a prompt to try. Use your own words if you like. Then tick the boxes when you've checked it worked.

Step 1: Start the site

Try asking“Make me a new website about [my topic]. It needs a big heading, two sections of words, a list of my top 3, and a link to another website. Put it in a new GitHub repo called [name].”

Step 2: Make it look like you

Try asking“Use [colours] and a fun font. Make the buttons change colour when I hover over them. Make sure it looks good on a phone.”

Step 3: Add a photo

Try asking“Add this photo to my site, in an images folder, with alt words that describe it.” (Attach the photo.)

Step 4: Put it in the shop window

Try asking“Host my site on Vercel and connect it to my GitHub repo, so it updates by itself.”

Bonus: a button that does something

Try asking“Add a button that shows a random fun fact about [my topic] when I click it.”

3. Rules

4. Hand it in

When it's finished, send your teacher your live website link (the .vercel.app one).

Before you send it, open it on a phone and check everything works.

Stuck?

Tell Claude exactly what's wrong and what you expected. For example:

My photo doesn't show

“My photo doesn't show on the live site. Can you find out why and explain it simply?” (Phone photos saved as .HEIC often don't show: ask Claude to change it to JPG.)

My live site didn't change

“I changed my site but the .vercel.app link still shows the old one. Why?” (Often the change is still on a branch and needs merging.)

Claude did something I didn't want

“Please undo that last change.” Nothing is lost: GitHub keeps every version.

Part 2

Make your NeoLife site even better

Your NeoLife site is really good. The staff system keeps patients' details private, and only staff can sign in. Well done!

We tested every button with pretend patients and found some things to fix. Each one has a simple why, a prompt to give Claude, and boxes to tick when you've checked it works.

Test with pretend patients only. All the pretend patients have phone numbers starting with +256 700 000. Never type in a real person's details to test.

On the website everyone sees

1. Fill in the empty boxes

WhyYour site still says [Phone number], [Street address] and [Email address]. It's like a birthday card that says “Dear [name]”. People can't call or find the clinic yet.

Try asking“Replace the placeholders with the clinic's real phone number, address and email: [the real details]. Also fill in the answers about insurance and how to pay.”

2. Tell people the real emergency number

WhyThe red box says “call your local emergency number”. But if someone is very sick and scared, they need the actual number to call, right there.

Try asking“In the emergency box, show this emergency number as a big button people can tap: [number the clinic gives you].”

3. Write the opening hours in just one place

WhyThe opening hours are written in four places: the table, the “call to book” box, the bottom of the page, and the JavaScript. It's like writing your birthday on four cards. If it changes, you have to remember to fix all four, and if you forget one, people get confused.

Try asking“The opening hours are written in four places. Keep them in one list in script.js and fill in all the other places from it. Explain how it works simply.”

4. Use the clinic's clock, not the visitor's

WhyThe “Open now” sign looks at the clock on the visitor's phone. If someone in London looks at your site at 9 in the morning, it's already 12 in Kampala, but the sign thinks it's 9. The clinic is in Kampala, so the sign should use Kampala time.

Try asking“Make the Open now / Closed now sign use Kampala time (East Africa Time), whatever country the visitor is in.”

In the staff system

5. Stop birthdays from the future

WhyWe registered a pretend patient born in the year 2030, and the system said “OK!” and called him 0 months old. Nobody can be born in the future. The form should say “That can't be right” when a date is wrong.

Try asking“In the staff system, don't allow a date of birth in the future. Also don't allow a follow-up date in the past, or an admission or discharge time in the future. Show a friendly message when a date is wrong.”

6. Blood pressure must look like blood pressure

WhyWe typed “banana” as a blood pressure, and the system saved it! Blood pressure is always two numbers with a line between them, like 120/80. A doctor reading “banana” wouldn't know what to do.

Try asking“Only let staff type blood pressure as two numbers with a slash, like 120/80. Show a helpful message if they type something else.”

7. Count the days the same way everywhere

WhyFor the same patient, the Dashboard says Day 4, but the Inpatients page says 3 days. It's like two clocks in the same room showing different times: which one do you believe?

Try asking“The Dashboard and the Inpatients page count a patient's days differently. Make them use the same counting, and label it clearly, like ‘Day 4’ everywhere.”

8. Make the tables fit on a phone

WhyOn a phone, the tables are too wide, so some columns hide off the side of the screen. Nurses often use phones, and they might not know they have to swipe sideways to see the rest.

Try asking“On phones, show each row of the tables as a small card instead, so nothing is hidden off the side.”

9. Warn about the same patient twice

WhyIf the same person is registered twice, their visits get split between two files, like having half your homework in one book and half in another. The doctor might miss something important, like an allergy.

Try asking“When staff register a new patient, check if someone with the same phone number, or the same name and birthday, already exists. If so, show them and ask ‘Is this the same person?’”

One fix at a time. After each one, test it, save it with a clear note, and check the live site. When they're all done, send your teacher your NeoLife link.
