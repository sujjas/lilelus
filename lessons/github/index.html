GitHub: the magic recipe book

By the end, Lizzie can say what a repo, commit, branch and pull request are, and she will have changed the words and added a real photo on mum's landing page herself, signed in to her own GitHub account.

Before class

about 15 min, on your own

Heads-up about preview links. When you open a pull request, Vercel posts a Preview link. New Vercel projects protect previews behind a Vercel login, so it will open on your laptop but not on Lizzie's device. Show it on your screen. To make previews public, go to the Vercel project's Settings → Deployment Protection.

The idea

10 min

Start with something she already knows: mum's recipe book. Now imagine a magic one that remembers every version of every recipe, ever.

Use her GitHub page for this part. Go through The words, in cake language together, then let her play Try it: bake a change. It's a pretend recipe book where nothing can break, and it shows the whole plan below before she does it for real.

Say it like this: “GitHub is a magic recipe book for websites. Every time we save a change, it keeps the old page too, so we can never really lose anything.”

Then add the last piece: Vercel watches the real book. Whenever main changes, Vercel bakes the website again, and the live site updates by itself.

This is today's whole plan in one picture. Show it at the start, then again at the end once she's done it.

The golden rule: this repo is public, so anyone in the world can read it, forever. Never put passwords, secret codes or anyone's private phone number in it. (Mum's shop WhatsApp number is fine: it's meant for customers and is already on her site.)

Quick check before building: “If you break something on a practice branch, does the live site break?” (No. Only main goes live.)

The build

45 min

Each step is one dot in the history. Before every click, Lizzie predicts what will happen. She does the clicking on her own laptop, signed in as herself. You guide and ask the questions.

Today she changes mum's real landing page. Two changes, both on one practice branch: some words (the copy) and one real photo. Nothing reaches mum's live site until you approve her pull request, so mistakes are safe to make.

  1. step 1 · 6 min

    Tour the recipe book

    She clicks
    1. Open the repo.
    2. Scroll slowly to the README at the bottom.
    She checks
    • Finds mum's site in the main folder: index.html, styles.css, script.js.
    • Opens the images folder and reads its README: the rules for photos.

    Predict“Which of the three files holds the words on the page: HTML, CSS or JavaScript?” (HTML, so index.html.)

  2. step 2 · 5 min

    Time travel through the history

    She clicks
    1. Click the commits link (top right of the file list).
    2. Open the commit called Move mum's site into this repo.
    She checks
    • Reads the sticky note (the commit message).
    • Explains the colours: green = added, red = removed.

    Predict“If we change one sentence, how many red and green lines will we see?” (One red: the old sentence. One green: the new one.)

  3. step 3 · 8 min

    Change the words (copy) on a practice branch

    She clicks
    1. Open index.html in the main folder and click the pencil (Edit).
    2. Find the words she's changing. The big headline is on the line starting <h1>; the line under it starting <p class="lede"> is the welcome sentence.
    3. Change only the words between the tags. Leave the < > parts alone.
    4. Click Commit changes…, choose Create a new branch, name it lizzie-photo-and-words, then Propose changes.
    She checks
    • Uses the words she and mum agreed (from the client interview), like a new headline or a better description of a cake.
    • Writes the sticky note: “Change the headline to …”

    Predict“Has mum's live site changed yet?” Then refresh lilelus.vercel.app to check. (No. It's only on the practice branch.)

  4. step 4 · 12 min

    Add a real photo

    She clicks
    1. Switch to her branch: the branch menu (it says main) → lizzie-photo-and-words.
    2. Open the images folder → Add file → Upload files → drag in the photo (for example birthday-cake.jpg). Commit straight to her branch.
    3. Back in index.html, find the Birthday & celebration card. Replace its drawing line, the one starting <svg class="ill", with:
      <img src="images/birthday-cake.jpg" alt="…">
    4. Commit to the same branch.
    She checks
    • Picks a photo mum agreed to share: cake only, no faces, under 1 MB.
    • Writes the alt words: what's in the photo, for people who can't see it.
    • Makes sure the file name in src matches the uploaded file exactly.

    Predict“What happens if the file name in src has a typo?” (The photo won't show. You'll see a broken image or just the alt words.)

  5. step 5 · 8 min

    Open a pull request and get it checked

    She clicks
    1. Click Compare & pull request, give it a title like New headline and a real birthday cake photo, then Create pull request.
    2. Open the Files changed tab.
    3. Wait about a minute for the Vercel bot. You open its Preview link on your laptop and show her.
    She checks
    • Finds all three changes: the words (red and green lines), the new photo file, and the swapped <img> line.
    • Looks at the preview: does the photo fit the card, and do the words read well?

    Predict“Why does this pull request have three commits, not one?” (One for the words, one for uploading the photo, one for showing it. Each save is its own sticky note.)

  6. step 6 · 6 min

    Approve, merge, and see it on mum's site

    She clicks
    1. You approve: Files changed → Review changes → Approve.
    2. She clicks Merge pull request, then Confirm merge.
    She checks
    • Refreshes lilelus.vercel.app every 30 seconds until her words and photo appear.
    • Shows mum!

    Predict“Mum's customers will see this. Who had to say yes before it went live?” (You, the head baker, by approving the pull request.)

If something goes wrong

The live site didn't change after 2 minutes

Check the Vercel dashboard. If there's no new deployment, the GitHub link isn't working: open the project's Settings → Git and connect sujjas/lilelus. If there's a deployment marked Error, open it to read why. Meanwhile, use it as a teaching moment: “Real developers check the logs. Let's look.”

The Preview link asks for a Vercel login

That's Vercel's preview protection, so it's expected. Show the preview on your screen, or switch protection off under Settings → Deployment Protection.

The pencil (Edit) button is grey

She's signed out, or signed in to a different account. She should be signed in as Lizzie-bizi.

The photo doesn't show (broken image)

Check the file name in src="images/…" matches the uploaded file exactly, including capitals and the ending (.jpg vs .jpeg vs .png). Phones often save as .HEIC, which browsers can't show: export it as JPG first. Fix it with another commit on the same branch.

She typed the heading but the page looks broken

A tag was probably deleted by accident, like the </h1>. Look at the red and green lines in Files changed: the mistake will be there. Fix it with another commit on the same branch. It's great debugging practice.

How to follow her work

any time

Everything she does in the repo is recorded by GitHub under her own name. She can see the same records you can, so nothing is hidden from her.

GitHub words

repository (repo)
The magic recipe book: the whole project and all its files.
commit
Saving your changes, with a note saying what you changed.
commit message
That note. Keep it short and clear.
history
Every saved version, ever. Nothing gets lost.
diff
The red and green lines that show exactly what changed.
main
The real book. Vercel puts this one on the live site.
branch
A practice copy where you can try things safely.
pull request
Asking “can my change go into main?”
merge
Saying yes: the change joins main.
revert
Undoing a change, with a new commit.
copy
The words on a website.
upload
Adding a file, like a photo, to the recipe book.
alt text
Words that describe a photo for people who can't see it.

Questions

  1. In your own words, what is a commit?
  2. Why did we make a branch instead of changing main straight away?
  3. What happened to the live site when we merged, and why?
  4. Write a good commit message for: “I added a photo of the wedding cake.”
  5. What should the alt words say for your photo, and who are they for?
  6. Why should you never put a password or secret code in a public repo?
