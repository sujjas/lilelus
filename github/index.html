GitHub is a magic recipe book

Our website is made of recipes (the code). GitHub keeps them all in one book, and it remembers every version, ever. So you can try new things without being scared of breaking anything.

How Claude helps

Claude is your helper baker. You're the head baker, so you always decide.

  1. 1

    You ask

    Tell Claude exactly what you want. “Make the Add to box button green.”

  2. 2

    Claude writes it

    Claude changes the recipe for you, and explains what it did.

  3. 3

    You check

    Guess what will change, then look at the green and red lines. Does it match?

  4. 4

    You decide

    Happy? Save it and make a pull request. Not happy? Say no, or ask Claude to try again.

When Claude helps with a change, the sticky note says Co-Authored-By: Claude. That's how everyone can see who helped, a bit like signing a birthday card together.

Head baker rules

  • Read before you say yes. Always look at what changed.
  • One small change at a time. Small cakes are easier to check than giant ones.
  • Don't understand it? Ask. “Explain this like I'm new to coding” works great.
  • No secrets in the book. The recipe book is public, so never put passwords, secret codes or anyone's private phone number in it. (Mum's shop number is fine: it's meant for customers.)

Your missions

Do these in our real recipe book. They tick themselves when GitHub sees you've done them. It can take a minute, so refresh if you need to.

  1. …

    Ask the head baker

    Open your first pull request.

  2. …

    Change the words

    Change some of the copy (the words) on Mum's landing page, and get it merged.

  3. …

    Add a real photo

    Upload a photo of one of Mum's cakes to the images folder, show it on her landing page, and get it merged.

  4. …

    Into the real book

    Get one of your pull requests merged, so your change goes live.

  5. …

    Bake with your helper

    Get a change made with Claude's help into the real book.

  6. …

    Undo it

    Use Revert to take a change back out.

  7. …

    Five saves

    Have 5 of your saves (commits) in the real book.
